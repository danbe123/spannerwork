# SpannerWork

SpannerWork is a local marketplace for **tools, skills, and workspace**.

It helps people complete automotive and DIY projects by connecting them with nearby providers who can rent out tools, offer hands-on expertise, and make workshop or garage space available on demand. Instead of forcing users to buy expensive equipment, rely only on traditional garages, or search through fragmented local listings, SpannerWork brings those needs into a single platform.

At its core, SpannerWork is built around a simple idea: many people already have valuable tools, useful skills, or available space, and others nearby need access to them. SpannerWork makes those resources discoverable, bookable, and paid.

## Overview

SpannerWork combines three categories of local access:

- **Tools** — rent equipment without needing to buy it outright
- **Skills** — hire trusted local people for practical help
- **Workspace** — book garages, bays, workshops, and related space when needed

The platform is designed to reduce waste, lower costs, create earning opportunities, and make practical work easier to get done.

## What SpannerWork does

SpannerWork gives users a way to:

- find tools for one-off or occasional jobs
- hire local mechanics, tradespeople, and practical specialists
- access workshop or garage space without a permanent lease
- compare providers, pricing, and reviews
- book and pay through a single platform
- build trust through ratings, profiles, and transparent listings

For providers, SpannerWork creates a way to earn from assets and expertise they already have.

Providers can list:

- tools and equipment
- labour and hands-on services
- garages, bays, workshop space, and storage
- specialist knowledge or project support

## Who it’s for

SpannerWork is built for both sides of the marketplace.

### Seekers

People who need to get something done and are looking for:

- a specific tool
- practical help with a job
- a workspace to carry out repairs or projects
- a more affordable and flexible alternative to traditional service routes

### Providers

People who want to earn from:

- tools and machinery they already own
- workshop or garage space that would otherwise sit unused
- skills in mechanics, repairs, fabrication, DIY support, trades, or related practical work

## How it works

### For seekers

A typical user journey looks like this:

1. Describe what is needed
2. Browse or receive relevant local options
3. Compare pricing, reviews, and provider details
4. Book the tool, service, or space
5. Pay securely through the platform
6. Leave feedback after completion

### For providers

A typical provider workflow includes:

1. Create a profile
2. Add listings for tools, services, or space
3. Set pricing and availability
4. Receive bookings, enquiries, or quote requests
5. Complete the transaction through the platform
6. Build reputation through reviews and repeat business

### Skilled help

This may include:

- mechanics

- fabricators

## Why SpannerWork exists

Practical work is often harder and more expensive than it should be.

People regularly face problems like:

- needing an expensive tool for a short task
- struggling to find trustworthy local help
- paying inflated prices for straightforward jobs
- having nowhere suitable to work on a project
- owning useful tools or space but having no simple way to earn from them

SpannerWork exists to solve those problems by creating a local network around access, trust, and utility.

## Value proposition

SpannerWork is built around three main benefits.

### Access over ownership

Many tools and pieces of equipment are only needed occasionally. Renting them is often more practical than buying them.

### Local expertise

There is valuable knowledge and capability in local communities. SpannerWork helps surface that expertise and turn it into paid opportunity.

### Flexible workspace

Not everyone has a garage, workshop, or suitable place to work. On-demand access to space makes more jobs possible.

## Product principles

SpannerWork is shaped by a few clear principles:

- **local first** — useful nearby resources should be easier to find
- **practical by design** — the platform should solve real-world tasks, not just generate listings
- **community powered** — trust, reviews, and repeat use matter
- **flexible access** — users should be able to find tools, labour, or space depending on what the job requires
- **provider friendly** — people should be able to earn from what they already have
- **transaction led** — value is created when real jobs, rentals, and bookings happen

## Business model

SpannerWork follows a marketplace model.

In general, this means:

- users can join the platform without heavy upfront cost
- providers create listings and set their own pricing
- transactions happen through the platform
- the platform earns from successful marketplace activity rather than requiring a traditional subscription-first model

This keeps the product aligned with actual usage and successful outcomes.

## Positioning

SpannerWork sits between several traditional models, but it is not identical to any one of them.

It is not just:

- a directory
- a classifieds board
- a garage
- a tool rental chain
- a trades marketplace
- a booking engine

Instead, it combines elements of all of these into one platform focused on practical local work.

A useful way to think about SpannerWork is:

> a local access marketplace for tools, skills, and workspace
